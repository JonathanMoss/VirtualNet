# 📟 VirtualNet - Student User Guide

Welcome to **VirtualNet**! This guide will help you get up and running on your simulated tactical radio net. VirtualNet lets you practice military voice communications, callsign procedure, and radio logsheet keeping just like operating real field radios.

---

## 🎯 Quick Start Checklist

1. Get the **4-character Net Access PIN** from your instructor (Sunray).
2. Enter your **Nickname** and click **CONNECT TO NET**.
3. Wait on the landing card until Sunray assigns your **Callsign** (e.g., `R11` or `H10`).
4. Once unlocked, practice your radio procedure!

---

## 1. 🌐 Joining a Radio Net

- When you launch VirtualNet, you'll see the **Join Radio Net** screen.
- Type in the **Net Access PIN** provided for your training exercise (for example: `A3F9`).
- Enter your **Nickname** (e.g., `SAM` or `JOHN`).
- Click **CONNECT TO NET**.

> **Note:** Once connected, your screen will display **`AWAITING CALLSIGN ASSIGNMENT...`**. Don't worry—your radio controls will unlock automatically as soon as Sunray assigns your callsign!

---

## 2. 🎙️ How to Transmit (Push-to-Talk)

VirtualNet works like a real two-way tactical radio: **only one person can speak at a time**.

### To Speak:
- **Press and hold the SPACEBAR** on your keyboard (or click and hold the red **PTT** button on screen).
- Listen for the brief **start tone** (beep).
- Speak clearly into your microphone.
- Release the key/button as soon as you finish your message.

### Channel Discipline:
- **Listen before keying!** If another station is transmitting, your display will show **`RECEIVING`** or **`CHANNEL BUSY`**.
- If you try to press PTT while the channel is busy, you will hear a warning buzz and your transmission will be blocked. Wait until the station finishes before transmitting.

## 3. 📻 Military Voice Procedure Basics

Good radio procedure keeps communication fast, clear, and disciplined. Remember the **RSVP** rule:

- **Rhythm**: Speak in short, natural phrases—not word by word.
- **Speed**: Speak slightly slower than normal conversation so receiving operators can write down your message.
- **Volume**: Speak in a steady, confident tone—do not shout into the microphone.
- **Pitch**: Keep your voice slightly higher than usual for extra clarity through radio noise.

### Key Pro-Words (Procedure Words):

| Pro-Word | Meaning | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **THIS IS** | Identifies who is speaking. | *"R11 THIS IS H10..."* |
| **OVER** | I have finished speaking and expect a response from you. | *"...MESSAGE FOLLOWS, OVER."* |
| **OUT** | Conversation is finished. No response expected. | *"...NOTHING FURTHER, OUT."* |
| **ROGER** | Message received and understood. | *"ROGER, OUT."* |
| **WILCO** | Message understood and WILL COMPLY. | *"WILCO, OUT."* |
| **SAY AGAIN** | Request to repeat the last transmission. | *"SAY AGAIN ALL AFTER GRID, OVER."* |

> ⚠️ **CRITICAL RULE:** **NEVER** say "OVER AND OUT"! "OVER" means you expect a reply; "OUT" means the conversation is finished. They contradict each other!

## 4. 📝 Keeping Your Radio Logsheet

In military radio nets, operators log every important message transmitted or received.

### Message Fields:
1. **DTG (Date-Time Group)**: Automatically generated in military format (e.g., `061030Z AUG 26` = 6th of August at 10:30 UTC).
2. **FROM Callsign**: The callsign sending the message (e.g., `R11`).
3. **TO Callsign**: The intended receiving station (e.g., `CONTROL` or `H10`).
4. **Precedence**: Select the urgency level:
   - `ROUTINE` (R): Standard administrative / operational updates.
   - `PRIORITY` (P): Important operational traffic requiring fast delivery.
   - `IMMEDIATE` (O): Urgent tactical messages relating to ongoing action.
   - `FLASH` (Z): Critical emergency / contact reports (highest priority).
5. **Event / Message Text**: The core content of the transmission.
6. **Initials**: Your operator initials.

Click **LOG ENTRY** to add your record to the net log sheet.

---

## 5. 🎛️ Radio Controls & Reference Tools

- **VU Meter**: Monitors your mic input level. Speak so the green segments light up cleanly without clipping red.
- **Volume Slider**: Adjusts incoming station audio playback level.
- **Squelch Control**: Filters out background noise floor.
- **BATCO Reference**: Access the interactive BATCO encryption slider card on your dashboard for tactical grid code training.

## 🚀 Summary Checklist for Good Net Operators

- ✅ Always wait for the channel to be clear (`IDLE`).
- ✅ Hold PTT, wait for the start beep, then speak.
- ✅ End transmissions with **OVER** or **OUT**.
- ✅ Keep logsheet entries accurate and up to date.
- ✅ Have fun and practice clear communications!
