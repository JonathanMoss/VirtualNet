# Rules & Constraints: VirtualNet

This document outlines the strict business rules, operational constraints, and validation logic enforced by the VirtualNet application to ensure realistic training simulation.

---

## 1. Session Admission & Privacy Rules

- **Zero PII (Personal Identifying Information) Policy**:
  - The application holds **no passwords, emails, or personal accounts**.
  - Student database records are ephemeral, referenced only by their nickname for the duration of the active exercise session.
  - All local session variables and database logs for stations must be purged or anonymized upon session termination.
- **PIN-Based Access**:
  - Net Sessions are secured via a **4-character alphanumeric PIN** generated by the server.
  - Students must supply the correct active PIN and a Nickname to join.
- **Awaiting Assignment Lock**:
  - Upon joining, the student connection is marked `AWAITING_ASSIGNMENT`.
  - In this state, the client cannot emit or receive voice streams, view logsheets, or receive the roster updates.
  - The student gains full client features only after an instructor assigns them a tactical callsign.

---

## 2. Callsign Structure & Net Hierarchy (AC-70816-C Annex C)

- **Net Control Station (NCS)**:
  - Designated as `0` (Spoken as `ZE-RO`).
  - NCS Commanders are designated as `0A` (`ZE-RO AL-FAH`).
- **Primary Subordinate Stations**:
  - Company level HQ stations are allocated fixed call signs: `10`, `20`, `30`, `40` (Spoken as `WUN ZE-RO`, `TOO ZE-RO`, etc.).
  - Commanders of primary subordinates: `10A`, `20A`, `30A`, `40A`.
- **Sub-Units & Sections**:
  - Sections are allocated numerical suffixes from the range: `11` to `14`, `21` to `24`, `31` to `34`, `41` to `44`.
    - E.g., `11` is 1st Section (Spoken as `WUN WUN`), `12` is 2nd Section.
  - Suffixes `A-F` are appended for specific vehicles or appointments (e.g., `11A` - `WUN WUN AL-FAH` for Commander).
- **Callsign Indicators (CI)**:
  - A daily-changing letter prefix (except `Z`, which is reserved for helicopters).
  - E.g., if the day's CI is `R`, the station `11` is addressed as `R11` (Spoken as `ROMEO WUN WUN`). The control station `0` is never prefixed with a CI.

## 3. Half-Duplex Transmission Contention

To simulate a single-frequency radio, the server acts as the physical frequency channel and enforces a **half-duplex lock**.

- **Channel Lock Rule**:
  - When a station transitions to `TRANSMITTING`, the server acquires a lock on behalf of that station.
  - While the lock is active, the server ignores and blocks audio streams from all other standard stations.
- **PTT Override Rule**:
  - The station with `CONTROL` role (or Instructor) can bypass the channel lock.
  - If `CONTROL` initiates a transmission while a standard station is transmitting, the server:
    1. Immediately terminates the active standard station's transmission.
    2. Sends a notification to the cut-off client (transitioning them back to `Idle` or `Receiving`).
    3. Allocates the channel lock to `CONTROL` and broadcasts their audio.
- **Maximum Transmission Limit (Anti-Stuck PTT)**:
  - If a client holds PTT continuously for more than 60 seconds (configurable), the server automatically terminates the transmission, releases the lock, and sets the station to `Muted` or `Idle` to prevent a stuck key from locking the net.

---

## 4. Net States (Free vs. Directed Net)

VirtualNet supports two operational modes that govern who stations are allowed to contact.

- **Directed Net (Default Mode)**:
  - Stations **must** obtain permission from Net Control before transmitting messages to other sub-stations.
  - *System Behavior*: The client UI visualizes this mode, showing warning prompts if a student attempts to log or route a call directly to another sub-station without an initiating handshake from Control.
- **Free Net**:
  - Stations are authorized to contact any other station directly on the frequency without Control's prior permission.

## 5. Radio Log Integrity & Validation

To build accurate logging habits, the digital log sheet enforces professional standards:

- **Immutability of Saved Entries**:
  - Students **cannot delete** log entries.
  - If a correction is needed, the student must mark the entry as `CORRECTED` or append a correction line.
- **Time/DTG Integrity**:
  - The Time field is auto-populated with the current local or UTC system time when the log entry row is initialized.
  - Students can edit this field (to record a message that was received slightly earlier), but the system prevents setting a timestamp in the future.
- **Required Fields**:
  - A log entry cannot be finalized (saved/locked) without at least the `From`, `To`, and `Text/Event` fields populated.

## 6. Phonetic Pronunciation & Figures

The application client should provide training references and validate logs based on correct phonetic standards:

### Spoken Letters (Phonetic Alphabet)
`A` (AL-FAH), `B` (BRAH-VO), `C` (CHAR-LIE), `D` (DELL-TAH), `E` (ECK-OH), `F` (FOKS-TROT), `G` (GOLF), `H` (HOH-TELL), `I` (IN-DEE-AH), `J` (JEW-LEE-ET), `K` (KEY-LOH), `L` (LEE-MAH), `M` (MIKE), `N` (NO-VEM-BER), `O` (OSS-CAH), `P` (PAH-PAH), `Q` (KEH-BECK), `R` (ROW-ME-OH), `S` (SEE-AIR-RAH), `T` (TANG-GO), `U` (YOU-NEE-FORM), `V` (VIK-TAH), `W` (WISS-KEY), `X` (ECKS-RAY), `Y` (YANG-KEY), `Z` (ZOO-LOO).

### Spoken Figures
Numbers in call signs, grids, and time checks must be spoken digit-by-digit:
- `1` (WUN), `2` (TOO), `3` (TH-REE), `4` (FOE-ER), `5` (FIE-YIV), `6` (SIX), `7` (SEV-EN), `8` (ATE), `9` (NINE-ER), `0` (ZE-RO).

## 7. Standard Proword Actions

The application client should model and validate the use of the following core prowords:

- **Transmission Initializer**:
  - Format: `HELLO [Called Call Sign(s)] THIS IS [Sender Call Sign] [Message Body] [Ending Proword]`
- **Endings**:
  - `OVER`: Spoken when a reply is required immediately. PTT channel lock is released.
  - `OUT`: Spoken when the exchange is finished and no reply is expected. PTT channel lock is released.
- **Receipts**:
  - `ROGER`: I have received your last transmission satisfactorily.
  - `WILCO`: I have received your last transmission, understand it, and will comply.
- **Readability Reports**:
  - Initiated by `RADIO CHECK OVER`.
  - Replied to with `OK` (Satisfactory), `DIFFICULT` (Workable with difficulty), or `UNWORKABLE` (Unusable).
- **Operational Injects**:
  - `CONTACT WAIT OUT`: Indicates immediate contact/exchange of fire has occurred. Locks the channel for other standard stations.
  - `SIGHTING [Encoded Location]`: Enemy sighted, no exchange of fire.
  - `NO PLAY`: Prefixes and suffixes any real-world emergency message to distinguish it from exercise play.
